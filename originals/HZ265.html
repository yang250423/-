C_202605509EN.000101.fmx.xml Official Journal of the European Union EN C series C/2026/5509 3.11.2026 Action brought on 12 August 2026 – PDD Holdings v Commission (Case T-535/26) (C/2026/5509) Language of the case: English Parties Applicant: PDD Holdings Inc. (George Town, Cayman Islands) (represented by: F. González Díaz, A. Müller and J.-F. Keustermans, lawyers) Defendant: European Commission Form of order sought The applicant claims that the Court should: — annul the decision; — in the alternative, annul the decision insofar as it is addressed to the applicant; — in the further alternative, to annul in full or in part the fines imposed on the applicant ex Article 2 of the decision; and — order the European Commission to pay its costs and the applicant’s costs for the present proceedings. Pleas in law and main arguments In support of the action, the applicant relies on seven pleas in law. 1. First plea in law, alleging that the decision unlawfully imputes liability to PDD under Article 34(1) and (2) Regulation EU) 2022/2065, i.e., Digital Service Act (‘DSA’) (1) despite not having been the addressee of an Article 33(4) DSA designation decision, which is a prerequisite for the Commission to enforce DSA obligations against a specific entity. 2. Second plea in law, alleging that the decision breaches the fundamental principles of personal liability, legality, legal certainty, and the presumption of innocence by applying the notion of ‘single economic unit’ to expand the notion of ‘provider’ under the DSA. 3. Third plea in law, alleging that the decision was adopted in breach of essential procedural requirements and fundamental procedural rights. The Commission unduly and disproportionately restricted Whaleco’s access to the file on the basis of an unlawful Article 5 of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/1201 (2). The Commission, moreover, based its assessment on an incomplete and unreliable Case file and failed to objectively and diligently review third-party evidence. Those defects affected the evidentiary process through which the Commission established the alleged infringement subsequently attributed to PDD and, individually and cumulatively, warrant annulment of the decision insofar as it concerns PDD. 4. Fourth plea in law, alleging that the decision errs in law by misinterpreting and misapplying Article 34 DSA. Rather than assessing whether the applicant diligently identified, analysed and assessed systemic risks, the Commission retroactively imposes a specific methodology which finds no basis in Article 34 DSA. It transforms the diligence obligation ex Article 34 DSA into an outcome-based requirement and imposes a de facto general monitoring obligation, contrary to Articles 6 and 8 DSA and the principle of proportionality. The decision also unlawfully equates certain alleged shortcomings in the drafting of the 2024 risk assessment report (‘2024 RAR’) to a breach of Article 34 DSA, without considering whether Whaleco in fact operated an effective and diligent systemic risk identification and assessment and breaches the fundamental principle of legal certainty by unlawfully relying on ex post requirements which were neither clear nor foreseeable. 5. Fifth plea in law, alleging that the Commission fails to discharge its burden of proof to the requisite legal standard, by, in particular, first unlawfully reversing the burden of proof, and second, relying on anecdotal and uncorroborated third-party sources which are methodologically and statistically flawed to such a degree that it renders the sources uninformative and unreliable. 6. Sixth plea in law, alleging that the decision’s finding that Whaleco breached Article 34 DSA is vitiated by clear errors of fact and assessment, since, in particular, the 2024 RAR employed a platform-specific methodology, diligently calculated risk levels based on reliable platform-specific and external data, and diligently assessed whether and how each of the Article 34(2) DSA influencing factors could impact the identified systemic risks. The decision further errs in summarily dismissing the relevance of the 2025 Interim Risk Assessment Report. 7. Seventh plea in law, alleging that the fine of EUR 200 million is unlawful and manifestly disproportionate, particularly given the novelty of the DSA regime, which warranted no or a purely symbolic fine. The decision furthermore erred in characterizing the alleged infringement as ‘particularly serious’; failing to properly account for Whaleco’s cooperation; considering a fine to be necessary for the purpose of deterrence; and calculating the statutory maximum by reference to the applicant instead of Whaleco. (1)  Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 19 October 2022 on a Single Market for Digital Services and amending Directive 2000/31/EC, OJ 2022, L 277, p. 1. (2)  Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/1201 of 21 June 2023 on detailed arrangements for the conduct of certain proceedings by the Commission pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 of the European Parliament and of the Council, OJ 2023, L 159, p. 51.
